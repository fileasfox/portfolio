Project 06, real case, anonymised

The dashboard that counted double

A client's dashboard showed about 2.2 times more entries than the official report. Two figures for the same question mean nobody trusts either of them.

BigQueryView modellingLooker StudioValidation
2.2×initial overcount
3causes found
0difference in entries
< 0.1%difference in requests

Diagnosis: isolate before blaming the source

Events, not entities

The view counted every entry event. A guest who came in three times counted three times.

Uniques summed across days

It added up daily uniques to get a month. The total for a date range did not match the official report, which counts uniques per month.

A fragile test filter

It only caught “test” and “TEST”, and a CONCAT with null fields silently dropped whole rows.

The right grain

One row per booking, plus one row per hotel, month and entry locator, each type with its own columns.

One official definition per metric

MetricDefinition
RequestsDistinct count of the external locator
RoomsDistinct count of the internal locator (one request can have several)
EntriesDistinct count of locators entering the flow, only for valid entry types, per hotel and month
ConversionRequests / entries, as a calculated field. Never an average of percentages
Range ruleUniques per month, so that the total for a range = the sum of its months

How I solved it

  1. I fixed the reference: the accounts team's official report, with one specific hotel's figures month by month.

  2. I isolated the rows that differed between the two sources, instead of comparing totals only.

  3. I redesigned the view at booking grain and versioned it under a new name, without overwriting the old one.

  4. I delivered numbered SQL validation blocks, each with its expected result in a comment.

  5. I built the ratios as calculated fields in Looker Studio, named in the client's own language.

Result

Entries identical to the official report across the whole hotel group, and requests within 0.1%, a gap explained by the test filter. The client and the accounts team are looking at the same figure again.

Where it applies

Any company with more than one dashboard: reconcile against a reference before building, fix the grain and keep one definition per metric.
