How I present data

A good analysis that is badly presented changes no decisions. Here are six charts I often see in reports, each next to the version I would deliver, using the same real Mallorca data and the reason for every change.

Four rules I always apply

The title is the conclusion

“Price by municipality” describes the chart. “Palma is a third cheaper than the island” says what to do with it.

One idea per chart

If two ideas are needed, that means two aligned charts, not one with two axes.

Colour signals

Grey for context and a single colour for what matters. The reader should know where to look without reading the legend.

The figure with its benchmark

Against the total, the previous period or the target. A number on its own is neither good nor bad.

A one-page report for management

This is how I hand a finding to someone with two minutes: the conclusion at the top, three figures, one chart, what I recommend and what I do not know. Everything else goes in the appendix.

To: Commercial managementFrom: Matteo EirasData: July 2026

A sea view justifies charging 27% more. A pool, 19%.

Comparing Mallorca listings of the same size, area, type and date, these two attributes support the highest price premiums. It is the same logic as the surcharge for a room with a view in a hotel.

What I recommend

  1. Price attributes separately, not only by room category.
  2. Test a view surcharge between 15% and 25%, below the estimated effect so as not to lose conversion.
  3. Measure upgrade conversion for one month before and one month after the change.

What I do not know yet

  • These are published prices, not prices paid.
  • It covers a single summer: the premium may change in low season.
  • It is a comparison with controls, not an experiment.
Source: Inside Airbnb, Mallorca, 14,817 listings. Full method and code in the project.

What I check before sending a chart

  1. Does the title state the conclusion? If someone only reads the title, do they know what is going on?
  2. Does it match the official source? At least one figure checked against a known reference.
  3. Is the benchmark there? Total, previous period or target, next to the figure.
  4. Is the aggregation right? Ratios as sum over sum; medians when there is a long tail.
  5. Do the bars start at zero? And if a line does not start at zero, make it obvious.
  6. Is anything superfluous? Borders, shadows, 3D, decimals and legends that could sit on the marks.
  7. Does it work without colour? For colour blindness, greyscale printing or a poor screen.
  8. Does it state the sample size and date? And what was left out.
