Design decisions

Every figure with its benchmark

A KPI on its own does not say whether it is good or bad. Each one shows the difference against Mallorca, in points or percent depending on the metric.

Medians, not means

Price and income have a long tail: a few luxury villas push the mean up. The median describes the typical listing.

Ratios aggregated correctly

Unavailable nights are stored as numerator and denominator. When filtering, both are summed and divided at the end; percentages are never averaged.

One meaningful colour

Red is the selection; grey is context. No decorative colours that send you looking for a legend.

Small-sample warnings

Below 30 listings the dashboard says so and fades the figures. A median of 8 homes is not enough to decide anything.

Cross-filtering

The map and the ranking also filter, like chart interactions in Looker Studio. The state is kept in the link so it can be shared.

How it is built

Preparation in pandas

dashboard.py reads the listings, the 5.4-million-night calendar and the reviews. It exports one row per listing (to compute medians when filtering) and a cube by municipality, host, size and month with numerators and denominators.

Reconciled before publishing

The script compares the cube total with a direct calculation on the calendar and stops if they differ. Mallorca's median price, €398, is the same as in the main project.

Looker Studio equivalent

A BigQuery source at the same grain, a calculated field SUM(no_disp) / SUM(noches), filter controls and cross-filtering enabled between charts.

What it does not show

An unavailable night is not the same as a booked one: it includes host blocks. That is why I do not call it occupancy. 84 calendar listings that are no longer in the listings file are left out.
