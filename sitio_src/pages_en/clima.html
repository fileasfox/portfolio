Tourism, heat and water in Mallorca

Can Mallorca handle its summer? I combined four official sources to measure how fast Palma is warming compared with the planet, how many hotel nights already fall on a tropical night, and what happens to the water every summer.

The two-minute summary

Palma is warming almost twice as fast as the planet

Since 1978, between 0.38 and 0.48 °C per decade depending on the source, against 0.21 °C for the planet. I give a range rather than one figure because the weather station has a shift of its own around 2020 (explained under “Data”).

Hotel nights have turned tropical

Between 2000-2003 and 2022-2025, the share of hotel nights falling on a night of 20 °C or more rose from 46% to 62%. Between half and two thirds of that rise is explained by the heat; the rest by tourism weighing more in the hot months.

Water is hitting lower lows

Every summer Mallorca's reserves lose 8.5 points on average. August 2026 is the lowest value in the ten-year series: 38.5%.

But hotel tourism does not explain the summer drop

In 2020, with hotels closed for much of the summer (92% fewer hotel nights from May to September), reserves fell more than in any other year: 11.7 points. What moves the water is winter rain and summer itself, not hotels.

Data: four official sources and a reproducible pipeline

One script downloads everything with caching and retries, loads it into SQLite and records source and date. Nothing is copied by hand.

What it does

Reads the official chart as data: the colour identifies the bars and the grid gives the exact scale.

How I know it is right

All 121 values match the labels printed on the chart once rounded, and August 2025 (41.3%) matches the Government's press release.

What it does

Finds the year the station changes level against a reference that is not affected by relocations or instrument changes.

Why it matters

The station jumped +2.2 °C in 1978, a relocation. Without this test, the warming trend would be heavily inflated.

What went wrong and how I caught it

  1. ERA5 does not work for Palma. I started with the reanalysis: its grid cells sit over the bay and gave minimum temperatures 4-5 °C above the station. I saw it when reconciling against AEMET's normals and switched to the official station.

  2. The station was relocated in 1978. 1975-1977 came out 3 °C below normal with almost no tropical nights. The homogeneity test confirmed it: the analysis starts in 1978.

  3. The API was mixing models. The first reference series changed suddenly in 2018: by default the API switches to another model from 2017. Asking for ERA5 only removed the jump.

  4. A step remains in 2020. The station drifts about 0.9 °C away from ERA5 from 2020. I cannot attribute it, so the key figures are given as a range between both sources.

Layered SQL with tests, as in dbt

The transformation follows the pattern data teams use: staging cleans each source, intermediate aggregates from day to month and the marts are the tables a dashboard reads. Every test is a query returning the rows that break a rule.

  1. RawWhat the APIs return, untouchedraw_*
  2. StagingTypes, names and wide format per sourcestg_*
  3. IntermediateFrom day to month: means, extremes and thresholdsint_*
  4. MartsMonth, year and hydrological year, BI-readymart_*
  5. TestsUniqueness, complete days, ranges and coverage4 of 4

Dependencies resolved automatically

The runner reads which tables each model uses and orders them (topological sort), just like dbt's ref(). No file numbering needed.

Ready for Looker Studio

The three marts are exported to CSV on every run: connecting them to Looker Studio, Power BI or Tableau takes minutes. They are in exports/.

Six in ten hotel nights are now tropical

Tourism has grown 20% since 2000 (from 41.1 to 49.2 million hotel nights) and is still concentrated in summer: in 2025, August had almost 40 times as many as January.

Water is hitting lower lows

Mallorca's reserves recover with winter rain and fall every summer. The last two low points are the lowest in the series.

What a destination or a hotel chain could do with this

Track heat as a KPI

The share of hotel nights on a tropical night is a new metric that is easy to follow next to occupancy. It affects comfort, energy and reviews.

Shift demand to May and October

The tourism calendar adds about 4 points of exposure. Shoulder months have far fewer tropical nights and better water levels.

Watch winter, not just summer

What decides the water is the rain from October to April. A spring alert on the reserves' high gives months of margin.

Sell the cool

In project 02, air conditioning already adds 12% to the price. With more tropical nights, that feature will matter more.

What this project shows

Ingestion from APIs and from a PDF, layered SQL with tests, statistics with intervals and homogeneity tests, reconciliation against official sources and a story that reads in two minutes. These are the skills that come up most in the postings I analysed.
