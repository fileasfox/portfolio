Account health and churn risk by ROI

Each hotel pays a fixed monthly fee. If what the product generates does not cover it, sooner or later the hotel cancels. The goal was to see it coming months in advance, not when the cancellation email arrives.

BigQuerySQLLooker StudioMetric definition

First: separate two money flows

What the hotel pays

A fixed monthly fee by hotel type, regardless of what the product generates. It is the cost of the product.

What the guest pays

Room, service and upgrade chosen by the end customer. For the hotel it is revenue, and it is what the product has to justify.

Mixing them up is the easiest mistake: measuring only upgrades, or treating the hotel's revenue as if it were ours. ROI compares the two.

Defining the metric

ConceptDefinition
Revenue generatedRoom price + upgrade + service, with each term set to 0 if missing, in EUR and excluding cancelled requests
Conversion to EURLatest rate per currency. The rate is in currency units per 1 EUR, so you divide by it
ROIRevenue generated in the month / monthly fee
Reading< 1× does not cover the fee (risk); ≥ 1× works; ≥ 2× is the target
Hotels on a variable modelWithout a fixed fee ROI does not apply: they are assessed on conversion (≤ 5% is a risk) and revenue

How I solved it

  1. I agreed the definition with the accounts team before writing any SQL: what counts as revenue and what does not.

  2. I built the currency conversion with the latest rate per currency and checked the direction of the rate against a known amount.

  3. I excluded cancelled and test bookings with a null-tolerant filter.

  4. I validated the fee table: every ID exists in the hotel master table, and it surfaced active accounts with no fee recorded.

  5. I crossed ROI with conversion and classified each hotel with explicit rules.

  6. I delivered it in Looker Studio, filterable by month and hotel, with the likely cause for each account manager.

Result

A monthly account-health view with three levels and a likely cause (low traffic, low conversion or low prices). The accounts team moves from reacting to a cancellation to preparing the conversation before the renewal.

Where it applies

Any B2B SaaS: value received against price paid, per account and per month. Customer Success, Revenue Operations and Account Management.
